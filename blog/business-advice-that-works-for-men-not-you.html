The Business Advice That Works Great for Men
and Terribly for You

Most popular business advice was built for a nervous system that never had to manage rejection, visibility, or worth the way yours does. Here's what to build instead.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 10, 2026 · 7 min read

You have followed the advice.

Post every day. Hustle in silence. Never explain, never apologize. Treat your business like a machine and optimize every function. You have read the books, listened to the podcasts, taken the courses built by men who built empires on relentless output and zero self-doubt.

And you have burned out trying to run their playbook in your body.

Here is what nobody told you: most popular business advice was built by and for a specific kind of nervous system. One that has spent a lifetime being rewarded for taking up space, for being direct without apology, for treating rest as weakness and relentlessness as virtue. That advice is not wrong. It is just not built for you.

✦

The Advice Was Never Neutral

"Just be more aggressive in your outreach." "Stop being so nice." "If you're not uncomfortable, you're not growing." These lines get repeated as universal truth. But they came from a specific conditioning: one where assertiveness was never punished, where directness was never called "difficult," where ambition was never quietly reframed as unlikeable.

You were not conditioned that way. Most women were not. You learned early that too much visibility invited criticism, that directness could cost you relationships, that wanting more needed to be softened before it was safe to say out loud. So when someone hands you a strategy built for a nervous system that never had to manage all of that, and it does not work, the problem is not your discipline. The problem is the fit.

Your Nervous System Is Not the Obstacle

This is the part that gets missed. When "just push through it" advice fails you, it does not mean you need more grit. It often means your body is doing exactly what it was trained to do: scan for danger before it lets you be seen. That scanning is not weakness. It is nervous system support your business genuinely needs to account for, not override.

"The advice was never wrong for the person who wrote it. It was just never written for you."

Growth that ignores your nervous system does not create sustainable success. It creates a business that runs on your adrenaline until it cannot anymore.

✦

What Works Instead

The alternative is not "do less" or "want less." It is building a business model that works with your actual wiring instead of against it. That might look like:

Built for Your Wiring, Not Borrowed From Someone Else's

  • Selling with compassion instead of pressure, because pushy sales language triggers the same threat response in you that it does in your potential client.
  • Building visibility in a way that feels expansive instead of exposing, so you can be seen without your body treating it as danger.
  • Pricing from your worth instead of your fear, because "just charge more" advice ignores the self-worth work underneath the number.
  • Structuring your week around your actual energy, not a hustle schedule built by someone who has never had to regulate a nervous system in survival mode.

None of this is softer business. It is smarter business, built on the truth of who is actually running it.

The Real Root

Underneath "this advice doesn't work for me" is usually something deeper: a story that says the way you are naturally wired is a flaw to fix rather than a fact to design around. It is not. Your empathy, your intuition, your care for the people you serve: these are not liabilities the advice forgot to mention. They are the foundation of a business model built for you instead of borrowed from someone else.

You do not need a personality transplant to succeed. You need a strategy that was actually built with your wiring in mind.
