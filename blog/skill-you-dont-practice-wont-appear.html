Self-Leadership

The Skill You Don't Practice
Will Not Magically Appear

Confidence in sales doesn't arrive before the work. It's built by the work. Here's why the practice always comes first, and how to finally give it a place on your calendar.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 9, 2026 · 7 min read

A little loving accountability for anyone building a business, an offer, a service, a course, or a community.

If you say you want to get better at sales, but you never make time to actually learn it, that's the whole problem. Right there.

Not because you're lazy. Not because you're incapable. Not because you're "bad at business."

Because confidence doesn't appear from wishing. It comes from practice.

✦

What So Many Women Actually Want

A lot of women want to feel more confident making offers. They want to stop overthinking their price. They want to communicate their value clearly, without shrinking it or overselling it. They want to stop feeling awkward when they invite someone to work with them. They want sales to feel less heavy, less cringey, less like something they have to brace for.

That desire is beautiful. It's also not enough on its own.

Wanting the skill is not the same as building the skill. And this is exactly where so many women get stuck, not because they don't care, but because they're waiting for something that isn't coming.

The Loop You Might Be Stuck In

See if any of this sounds familiar.

You keep saying sales feels hard, but you don't schedule any time to learn a different way of doing it. You keep saying you want your business to grow, but you don't protect time to work on the skills that would actually grow it. You keep waiting to feel confident before you practice, when practice is the thing that builds the confidence.

This isn't a character flaw. It's a very human pattern. Most of us were never taught that competence comes before confidence, not the other way around. We were taught to wait until we felt ready. So we wait. And the waiting starts to feel like the plan.

Confidence is not a feeling you wait for. It's a skill you build by showing up before you feel ready.

✦

Why This Isn't Really About Sales

Underneath the sales avoidance, there's usually something else running the show. A nervous system that reads "asking for money" as a threat. A belief, often inherited, that wanting to be paid well makes you greedy or too much. A quiet fear that if you get good at this, you'll actually have to show up for it.

If any of that resonates, it's worth reading Why Asking for Money Feels Like a Threat (And What to Do About It), which goes deeper into what's actually happening in your body when you avoid pricing conversations or hesitate to make an offer.

That's worth naming honestly, because it changes what "practice" actually means. It's not about memorizing a script or forcing yourself to sound more confident than you feel. It's about giving your nervous system enough repetition that the whole thing stops feeling like danger. Regulation comes before skill. Skill comes before ease.

You don't need to become a different person to sell well. You need reps, in a container that feels safe enough to actually try.

What Practice Actually Looks Like

This is exactly the work we do inside Money & Growth for Women. Sales without the cringe. Money mindset without the shame. Offer clarity without overcomplicating everything you're already doing well. Self-leadership that doesn't ask you to turn into someone you're not.

Inside the free community, you'll also find my free course, Selling for Women Who Hate Sales. It's built to help you shift how you think about sales, money, confidence, communication, and your own value, one small rep at a time.

But here's the honest part, and I'd rather tell you the truth than sell you a shortcut: it can only help you if you actually use it. A course sitting unopened in your inbox doesn't build a skill. Neither does a book on your nightstand or a podcast episode you meant to finish.

So if you're a woman who keeps saying "I need to get better at sales," consider this your invitation to stop waiting for the feeling and start practicing the skill.

✦

Give It a Place on Your Calendar

Growth isn't reserved for women with the most time. It's built by women who decide their growth gets a place on the calendar, even if that place is just thirty minutes this week.

Not someday. Not when it feels easier. Not when you finally feel ready.

This week. Thirty minutes. That's the whole ask.
