Money Mindset

If You Never Invest in Yourself,
It's Hard to Ask Others
to Invest in You

The way you buy often reveals something about the way you sell. A look at the hidden link between money mindset and confident pricing.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 4, 2026 · 7 min read

This might land a little uncomfortably. I think it matters anyway.

Here's something I see over and over with women building their own businesses, whether that's a coaching practice, a side gig, a service business, or a growing community. They're trying to sell their offers. But at the same time, they won't invest in the very support that would help them grow.

They look at a paid community, a course, a mentorship, a workshop, a skill-building space, and think:

"That's too much." "I can figure it out myself." "I'll join when I'm making more money." "I need to wait until things feel safer." "I can't spend money on that right now."

I understand this. Being thoughtful with money matters. Not every offer is the right offer, and not every stage calls for the same investment. But there's a difference between discernment and scarcity, and most women were never taught how to tell them apart.

✦

The Difference Between Discernment and Scarcity

Discernment asks a real question: Is this the right support for the stage I'm in?

Scarcity says something else entirely: I can't invest in myself until I'm already successful.

That belief becomes a trap. If you're unwilling to invest in your own growth, your own skills, your own clarity, your own confidence, your own next level, it becomes very hard to energetically stand behind the invitation for someone else to invest in you.

Not because you're bad at business. Because part of you may still believe that spending money on growth is risky, indulgent, unnecessary, or something you're only allowed to do "later," once you've already proven yourself.

That belief usually didn't start in your business. It started somewhere much earlier, in a family, a relationship, or a culture that taught you money was for saving, not for growing. This is where money mindset work and nervous system support for entrepreneurs actually meet. Your body learned a rule about money long before you ever had a business to apply it to.

"The way you buy often reveals something about the way you sell."

How This Shows Up in Your Selling

If scarcity is the energy underneath your own buying decisions, it doesn't stay contained there. It leaks into how you sell.

You hesitate to say your price out loud. You over-explain your offer before anyone's even asked a question. You assume people will think it's too expensive before they've said a word. You feel a flicker of guilt asking someone to pay you for something that took years to build. You project your own money fears onto potential clients who never voiced any of those fears themselves.

This is often at the root of why selling without feeling pushy feels so hard for so many women. It's not a script problem. It's not a personality problem. It's that you're trying to sell from a foundation you don't fully trust yourself.

This is why money mindset and sales are so connected, and why real confidence coaching for women starts here instead of with tactics or templates. You can't talk someone into a sale you don't fully believe they're allowed to make.

✦

Why This Matters Even More If You're Just Starting Out

If you're early in building a side business or a service-based practice, this pattern can be especially sneaky. You tell yourself you'll invest in support once the business is more established. Once there's more revenue. Once it feels safer.

But business coaching for women beginners so often reveals the opposite is true. The investment usually needs to come before the proof, not after it. Waiting for safety before you'll grow is exactly the scarcity pattern in disguise, just wearing a responsible-sounding outfit.

The Real Question to Ask Yourself

This isn't about spending money recklessly. Please don't hear it that way.

It's about asking a more honest question the next time you find yourself talking yourself out of an investment:

Ask Yourself

  • Am I making this decision from grounded discernment, or from scarcity?

One is a genuine read on timing, fit, and capacity. The other is a story your nervous system learned a long time ago, one that says growth has to be earned through struggle before it's allowed to be supported.

✦

What It Means to Invest From Self-Leadership

When you invest from self-leadership, you're not just buying information. You're choosing to become the kind of woman who backs her own growth, in feminine energy business terms, someone who trusts receiving as much as she trusts effort.

And the more you can back your own growth, the easier it becomes to invite other people to back theirs. Your prices start to feel less like a negotiation and more like a reflection of something you've already proven true for yourself.
