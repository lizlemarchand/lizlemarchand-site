The ONE Magic Method
That Guarantees Sales

Every coach claims to have found the one sales method that works. Here's why that belief might be the very thing keeping you stuck, and what actually works instead.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 11, 2026 · 8 min read

If you have spent any time online looking for the "right" way to sell, you have probably noticed something. Everyone is convinced they have found it.

The exact script. The five-step framework. The one funnel that changes everything. The post that goes, "Do this and you'll never struggle with sales again."

And if you're anything like the women I work with, you have tried more than one of these. You followed the steps. You said the words. And when it didn't work, a quiet, painful thought crept in.

Maybe I'm the problem.

I want to offer you something different today, because I think this belief, that there is one method that guarantees sales, might be one of the biggest things keeping women stuck in their business.

✦

There Is No Universal Recipe for Sales Success

Here is the truth nobody selling you a course wants you to hear: there is no one technique, one playbook, one script that works for every woman, every audience, every business.

What feels natural and effective for one woman might feel forced, misaligned, or completely draining for another. Not because she is doing it wrong. Because it was never built for her nervous system, her personality, or her people.

When we believe there must be one right way, we end up chasing the next shiny solution instead of building the one thing that actually moves the needle: trust in ourselves.

"The real shift is not finding the perfect method. The real shift is becoming willing to grow."

Why "Just Follow My Method" Sets You Up to Feel Like a Failure

This is why I have such a problem with coaches who promise that their exact method guarantees results.

Because when it doesn't work for you, and it won't, not perfectly, not for everyone, you don't walk away thinking "that method wasn't the right fit." You walk away thinking you are broken, bad at business, or not cut out for this.

That is not true. It never was.

If a method doesn't land for you, it means you gathered information. That's it. It doesn't mean you failed. It means you now know one more thing that isn't your way, which brings you closer to what is.

This matters especially for women who are just starting out, or who are rebuilding their confidence after trying something that left them feeling small. Business coaching for women beginners should teach self-trust first, technique second. Not the other way around.

Your Way Is Built, Not Found

Finding the way that works for you rarely happens through one lightning-bolt discovery. It happens through a much less glamorous process:

You try something. You notice how it feels in your body, not just whether it "worked." You tweak it. You try again. You keep what feels honest and natural. You let go of what feels heavy, performative, or like you're wearing someone else's skin.

This is what selling with compassion actually looks like in practice. Not a gentler script. A willingness to keep adjusting until the way you sell, show up, invite, and follow up feels like you.

That process, testing, noticing, adjusting, and continuing, is growth. It is also, quietly, the most reliable path to learning how to sell without feeling pushy. Pushy selling is often just someone else's method, forced through a body and a business it was never designed for.

✦

The Nervous System Piece Nobody Talks About

If your body tenses before you send a pitch, or you feel a wave of dread before a sales call, that is not a sign you need a better script. It is often nervous system support for entrepreneurs that's missing, not a new tactic.

Your nervous system remembers every time selling felt unsafe: the awkward ask, the rejection, the moment you felt "too much" for wanting to be paid. It makes sense that it would brace before you try again.

The women who eventually sell in a way that feels clear and grounded aren't the ones who found the perfect formula. They are the ones who slowed down enough to notice what their body was telling them, and built a way of selling that their nervous system could actually trust.

The Question to Ask Instead

Next time you catch yourself thinking, "Maybe this next program will finally fix everything," pause and ask yourself:

Am I looking for this because I'm genuinely curious? Or because I don't trust myself yet?

There is nothing wrong with learning new things. But if you notice you're collecting method after method, hoping one of them will finally be the one, that search itself is worth looking at. Often it isn't a knowledge gap. It's a self-trust gap.
