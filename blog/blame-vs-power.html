Self-Leadership

The Difference Between
Blame and Power
(And Why You Can't Have Both)

Blame explains your pain, but it can't build your future. Learn the real difference between fault and personal responsibility, and how to reclaim your power without denying what happened to you.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 18, 2026 · 7 min read

You did not cause what happened to you.

You may have been hurt. Betrayed. Dismissed. Manipulated. Abandoned. Treated unfairly by someone who should have known better. None of that was your fault, and nothing about taking responsibility for your life requires you to pretend otherwise.

But here is the truth bomb most people are not ready to hear: blame may explain your pain, but you cannot create a powerful life from the seat of the victim.

✦

Why Personal Responsibility Feels Like an Accusation

So many women flinch at the words "personal responsibility" because they hear something underneath them: so you're saying this was my fault?

No. That is not what this means, and the confusion between the two is exactly what keeps so many women stuck.

Fault and responsibility are not the same word wearing different clothes. Fault looks backward and asks who caused this. Responsibility looks forward and asks who gets to decide what happens next. You can hold both truths at once: what happened to you was not okay, and what happens from here is yours to author.

Blame Keeps Your Power Somewhere Else

Blame feels protective because it locates the problem outside of you. But it comes with a hidden cost: it also locates your power outside of you.

When your peace depends on someone else changing, apologizing, understanding, or finally seeing what they did, you have handed your future to a person who may never deliver it. You are not wrong to want acknowledgment. You are just waiting on something you cannot control to unlock something you can.

"You may not be responsible for what happened, but you are responsible for what happens next."

That single distinction is the hinge everything else swings on.

✦

What Self-Leadership Actually Asks of You

Taking your power back does not mean minimizing your pain or excusing someone else's behavior. It means asking a different set of questions, ones that put you back at the center of your own story:

Questions Worth Asking Yourself

  • What can I choose now?
  • What boundary do I need to set?
  • What pattern am I ready to stop repeating?
  • What support do I need to move forward?
  • What would it look like to lead myself from here?

These questions do not erase what happened. They simply stop letting what happened make every decision for you.

You Can Hold Both Truths

This is not about becoming hard, detached, or pretending you were never hurt. Self-leadership and self-compassion are not opposites.

You can acknowledge that something was unfair and still refuse to live from helplessness. You can honor your pain and still take your power back. You can tell the truth about what happened to you and still become the woman who decides what happens next.

That is not self-blame. That is self-leadership.

✦

The Question Worth Sitting With

Where are you still waiting for someone else to change before you let yourself move forward? What would shift if you decided, today, that you did not need their apology, their understanding, or their permission to start choosing yourself?

You do not need to have all the answers to start. You just need to stop waiting.
