Nobody Has Ever Looked Back
and Been Glad They Worried

Worry has never once prevented the thing you were afraid of. It has only ever made you suffer through it twice.

By Liz Lemarchand · July 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Think of the last thing you worried about for weeks. The client who might say no. The launch that might flop. The conversation you were sure would go badly.

Now ask yourself honestly: after it was over, whichever way it went, did you ever think, "I'm so glad I spent all that energy worrying"?

You didn't. Nobody does. Not once, not ever, has a woman looked back on a worry spiral and felt it earned its keep. And yet most of us keep doing it anyway, as if this time the worry will be the one that pays off.

✦

Worry Feels Like Work, But It Isn't

Worry is sneaky because it feels productive. Your mind is busy, your body is activated, you are clearly doing something about the problem. It has all the physical markers of effort without any of the outcomes of effort.

Here's the test: worry rehearses the problem. Planning solves it. If what you're doing right now is imagining the bad outcome again and again without landing on a next step, that isn't preparation. That's your nervous system spinning in place, burning fuel and going nowhere.

Why You Do It Anyway

This pattern rarely comes from nowhere. If you learned early that being caught off guard was dangerous, whether that meant a parent's mood, a financial scare, a betrayal you didn't see coming, your body adopted a strategy: stay alert to every possible bad outcome, and maybe you'll never be blindsided again.

The problem is that this strategy has a fatal flaw. Worry doesn't actually prevent bad outcomes. It just makes sure you suffer through them twice, once in your imagination and once if they happen at all. Your nervous system isn't broken for doing this. It's doing exactly what it was trained to do. The training is just outdated.

Worry has never once prevented the thing you were afraid of. It has only ever made you suffer through it twice.

✦

What to Do Instead of Worrying

You can't just tell yourself to stop worrying. That's like telling a smoke alarm to be quiet while the room is still full of smoke. What actually works is giving your nervous system something real to do instead of spinning.

Separate the worry from the work

Ask: is there an action available to me right now? If yes, take it, even a small one, and let that count as done. If no action is available, name that too. "There is nothing I can do about this right now" is a complete, honest thought, and it's one your mind can actually rest on.

Give worry a scheduled time and place

Set aside fifteen minutes a day as your official worry window. When a worry shows up outside that window, you don't have to fight it, just tell it its appointment isn't until later. Most worries lose their urgency once they're not allowed to run the whole day.

Build evidence instead of predictions

Worry lives in the future, in a story you're making up. Pull yourself back into what's actually true right now. What is happening in this moment? What has actually gone wrong versus what you're imagining might go wrong? This is one of the simplest forms of nervous system support for entrepreneurs, because it interrupts the loop with fact instead of feeding it with fiction.

Trade certainty for capability

You will never worry your way into knowing how something turns out. What you can build is trust in your own ability to handle whatever does happen. That shift, from "I need to know it will be okay" to "I know I can handle it if it isn't," is where real confidence coaching for women actually starts. Not the fake-it-till-you-make-it kind. The kind that's built on evidence you've gathered about yourself over time.

✦

The Root Underneath It

Worry often isn't really about the client, the launch, or the conversation at all. It's about an old belief that you have to stay one step ahead of disaster or it will catch you. That belief may have made sense once. It doesn't have to run your business, or your life, anymore.

You are allowed to meet the future as it comes instead of pre-living every version of it that could go wrong. That isn't recklessness. It's what it looks like to finally trust yourself.
