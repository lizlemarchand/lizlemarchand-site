Everyone's Talking About AI Replacing Coaches.
Here's the Question No One's Asking.

The fear that AI will replace coaches isn't really about technology. Here's the question underneath it that almost nobody is willing to ask.

By Liz Lemarchand · September 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Every week there's a new post about AI coming for coaches. AI can write your content. AI can build your funnel. AI can answer client questions at 2am. AI can do in thirty seconds what used to take you an hour.

Some women read that and panic. Others get defensive and insist AI could never replace what they do. Both reactions are pointing at the same unexamined question, and it isn't the one everyone's actually arguing about.

The real question isn't "can AI do what I do." It's "did I believe my presence was irreplaceable before AI ever showed up?"

That's a much less comfortable thing to sit with.

✦

What AI Actually Can and Can't Do

I use AI in my own business. I built a tool with it. So I'm not writing this from a place of fear or denial about what it's capable of, and I'm not interested in either the hype or the doom.

Here's what's true: AI can generate a solid framework, draft a decent email, spot a pattern in language, summarize a call. It can do a lot of the informational, structural work that used to feel like the hard part of running a coaching business.

Here's what's also true: AI cannot feel the shift in a client's voice when they say "I'm fine" and mean the opposite. It cannot sit in the two seconds of silence after someone almost cries and know whether to speak or wait. It cannot be changed by the person in front of it. Whatever it reflects back to you, it isn't risking anything to say it.

That difference is not a technicality. It's the entire relationship.

The Fear That Was Already There

Here's the part I actually want to talk about, because I think it's the part everyone's skipping past.

For a lot of women, the fear that AI will make them replaceable isn't a new fear. It's an old one, wearing a new outfit. Long before any of this, there was already a quiet voice asking whether what you offer is really worth what you charge for it, whether anyone would still want you if they had a cheaper option, whether your value comes from something as easily copied as your words.

AI didn't create that doubt. It just gave it a very specific, very current thing to attach itself to.

That's worth noticing, because it means the fix isn't proving you're smarter than a language model. You can't out-argue a fear that was never really about the technology. The actual work is the same work it's always been: deciding, on your own terms, what your presence is worth when it can't be automated, and believing that before you have to defend it to anyone.

The fix isn't proving you're smarter than a language model. It's deciding what your presence is worth before you have to defend it to anyone.

What Makes the Work Yours

I built the Permission Signals Detector because I wanted a tool that could flag a permission-seeking sentence in someone's bio or sales page in seconds. That's a genuinely useful thing for AI to do well.

What it can't do is sit with a woman while she figures out why she keeps writing that way. It can't notice that she's been apologizing for her prices since she was twenty-six and connect that to the way she talks about her mother's business failing. It can't feel her nervous system settle when she finally says the price out loud without flinching. That part was never information. It was presence, timing, and a kind of attention that has to be lived, not generated.

That's the part of your work that isn't going anywhere. Not because AI isn't advancing, but because it was never competing for that part in the first place.

The Real Question

So instead of asking whether AI will replace you, a more honest question might be: what have you been leaning on to prove your worth that was always replaceable? A framework. A script. A polished piece of content. Those things were always going to be reproducible by something, eventually. They were never what actually held your clients.

What held them was you noticing what they couldn't say out loud yet. That's still yours. It was always going to require a body, a nervous system, and a willingness to actually be affected by another person, none of which any tool has, no matter how good it gets.

If this is the kind of question you want to keep sitting with, that's exactly what the newsletter is for. No hype, no doom, just honest thinking about what's actually yours to build.
