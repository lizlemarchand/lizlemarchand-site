Nervous System Regulation Is
a Practice, Not
a One-Time Fix

Nervous system regulation doesn't work if you only reach for it once you're already triggered. Here's what actually builds a practice that holds under pressure.

By Liz Lemarchand · September 1, 2026 · 7 min read

I love my children more than anything. And sometimes, I don't love being a mom.

Especially during the teenage years.

Before I had teenagers, I thought that because I understood communication, emotional intelligence, and nervous system regulation, I would know how to handle the hard moments. I coach women on this. I teach it. I have language for it.

But sometimes I get triggered. I lose my patience. I raise my voice. I react with an intensity I regret almost immediately.

I know better, and yet I don't always do better.

Then comes the guilt. Guilt because I work so much. Guilt because I don't make enough time for them. Guilt because even when we're together, I'm not always fully present.

My children are a major part of my Big Why. So much of what I'm building, in my business and everywhere else, is about creating security, possibility, and a better future for them. But sometimes I get so focused on building for them that I forget to simply be with them.

Recently I had one of those moments when I didn't respond as the mother I want to be. And it gave me a very clear reminder, one that applies just as much to how I lead my business as it does to how I parent.

✦

Nervous System Regulation Isn't Something You Reach for After You're Triggered

By the time you're in it, your body has already taken over. You're no longer calmly choosing from a list of tools you know. You're reacting from whatever capacity you built before the moment arrived.

This is the part I think a lot of women in business get wrong, myself included. We treat regulation like a fire extinguisher. Something we grab when the meeting goes badly, when the client gets short with us, when the launch doesn't land, when a difficult conversation about money is staring us down. We think if we just know the breathing technique or the grounding exercise, we'll be able to use it in the heat of the moment.

But regulation doesn't work that way. It's not a tool you pick up cold. It's a pathway you build through repetition, long before you need it.

The pattern underneath the pattern

If you've been in business for any length of time, you already know this feeling. You show up to a sales call regulated and grounded, and something the client says lands wrong, and suddenly you're either shutting down or overexplaining. You know your pricing is fair, and then someone questions it, and your whole body tightens before your mind even catches up. You've done the work. You've read the books. You still get hijacked.

That's not a failure of knowledge. It's a failure of daily practice.

Knowing the tools isn't enough. You have to keep building the pathway back to them, in the quiet moments, so the pathway is already worn in when you need it under pressure.

The Inner Work: What Regulation Actually Requires

Regulation has to be a daily priority, not a crisis response. It happens in the quiet moments: through breathing, grounding, movement, rest, and creating space to check in with yourself before anything is on fire.

That daily practice is what makes the tools accessible when emotions run high. When you stop practicing regulation outside the difficult moments, you stop being able to reach for it inside them. This is true whether the trigger is your teenager slamming a door or a client questioning your worth in an email. The nervous system doesn't know the difference between a parenting moment and a business moment. It just knows whether it has a well-worn path back to steady, or not.

This is also why regulation can't be outsourced to a single technique you learn once in a workshop and file away. It's closer to physical fitness. You don't do one workout and stay strong for a decade. You show up for it consistently, especially on the days nothing is testing you, because that's what builds the capacity you'll need on the days something is.

✦

The Practical Work: Building a Practice That Holds

You don't need a complicated system. You need a few honest, repeatable check-ins.

A daily regulation practice

  • Notice your baseline before the day gets loud. A few minutes of breath or quiet before you open your inbox tells your body it's safe, before anything has asked it to prove otherwise.
  • Build in recovery, not just output. Movement, rest, and unstructured time aren't rewards for finishing the to-do list. They're what refills the capacity your business and your family both draw from.
  • Name what's happening while it's small. A tight chest during a pricing conversation or a short fuse by 5pm is information, not a character flaw. Catching it early is what keeps it from running the show later.
  • Repair instead of perform. When you do react from the dysregulated place, and you will, the practice isn't pretending it didn't happen. It's coming back, owning it, and reconnecting, with your kids and with your clients and team.

None of this is about becoming a person who never gets triggered. It's about becoming someone whose baseline is steady enough that when you do get pulled off center, you know the way back.

What This Has to Do With Leading a Business

I think this is one of the most under-discussed parts of building something real. We talk about strategy, offers, visibility, and sales. We don't talk enough about the fact that your nervous system is the operating system underneath all of it.

A dysregulated leader makes reactive decisions. She underprices out of fear, overexplains out of guilt, avoids the hard conversation, or says yes to the wrong thing because her body is in survival mode and survival mode just wants the discomfort to end. None of that is a strategy problem. It's a regulation problem wearing a strategy costume.

The women I work with often come to me thinking they need a better pricing formula or a better sales script. Sometimes they do. But just as often, what they actually need is a daily practice that keeps their nervous system regulated enough to use the strategy they already have. You can know exactly what to say and still not be able to say it if your body doesn't feel safe enough to.

✦

I Still Won't Get Every Moment Right

I won't. Not as a mother, and not as a leader. But I can take responsibility when I don't. I can apologize without excuses, repair the connection, and recommit to the practices that help me show up differently next time.

I cannot give my children, or my business, a perfect version of me. But I can give them a real one. One who loves what she's building fiercely, owns her mistakes, and keeps doing the work.

If you're building something right now, in your business or your family or both, and you keep finding yourself reacting from a place you don't recognize, hear this: it doesn't mean you've failed at regulation. It means regulation isn't a switch you flip. It's a floor you build, one quiet, unremarkable day at a time.
