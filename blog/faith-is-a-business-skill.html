Faith Is a
Business Skill

A launch that flops or a client who says no isn't proof you failed. It might just mean the story isn't finished yet.

By Liz Lemarchand · August 1, 2026 · 6 min read

One of the most powerful shifts I've ever made was this: I stopped deciding that something had gone wrong just because it didn't go my way.

That changed everything.

Because when you believe things are always working out, even when the path looks messy, slow, inconvenient, or confusing, you stop spiraling so fast.

The launch that didn't sell? Maybe it gave you the data you needed.
The client who said no? Maybe they were never your person.
The opportunity that fell apart? Maybe it cleared space for something better.
The delay? Maybe it protected you from building on the wrong foundation.

This is not toxic positivity. It's not pretending everything feels good. It's choosing not to hand your power over to the first disappointing plot twist.

That kind of faith is a superpower. And in business, it might be one of the most underrated skills there is.

✦

Why This Is a Nervous System Issue, Not a Mindset Issue

Here's what nobody tells you when they hand you a business plan: most business outcomes are neutral until your nervous system decides what they mean.

A launch that undersells is just a number, until your body reads it as proof you were never good enough to begin with. A client who says no is just one conversation, until your nervous system files it as evidence that you should shrink your offer, drop your price, or quit altogether. The event itself is small. The meaning you assign to it, often before you've even had a chance to think, is what determines what you do next.

Most people lose themselves the second life doesn't cooperate. They panic. They force. They overthink. They make dramatic decisions from temporary fear. And none of that is a character flaw. It's a nervous system doing what nervous systems do when they don't feel safe: looking for the fastest possible exit from uncertainty, even if that exit is a bad decision made in a hurry.

Faith is what interrupts that loop. Not because you've talked yourself into feeling calm, but because you've built enough trust with yourself that you don't need the whole staircase to appear before you'll take the next step.

✦

What Faith Actually Looks Like in Practice

Unwavering faith doesn't mean you never feel scared. It means fear is no longer the CEO.

When you trust that things are still unfolding for you, even when they don't look finished yet, you move differently.

You breathe before reacting, instead of firing off a discount code the moment a launch goes quiet.
You look for the lesson, instead of deciding the lesson is that you should never try again.
You stay open, instead of slamming a door on an idea because the first version of it didn't land.
You take the next step without needing proof that the whole plan will work.

None of this requires you to feel great about the setback in the moment. It just requires you to hold off on the verdict. A slow launch is only a failure if you decide, right now, in the rawest part of the disappointment, that it is one. Most of the time, you don't actually know yet what it was for.

Why Women in Business Need This More Than Most

If you've spent years being taught to read every misstep as a referendum on your worth, this skill is going to feel unfamiliar at first. That conditioning runs deep, and it doesn't just tell you that you made a mistake. It tells you that the mistake means something about who you are.

Faith as a business skill asks you to separate those two things. What happened is data. What you decide it means about you is a choice, even when it doesn't feel like one.

This is also why doing this alone is so much harder than doing it inside a community of women who are further along, who can remind you in real time that a quiet week is not a verdict, that a client who said no was not a rejection of your worth. Faith gets easier to practice when you're not the only one holding it.

What Actually Changes

Honestly, this shift has changed my life more than almost anything else. Not because everything always looks perfect. It doesn't. But because I no longer need everything to look perfect in order to trust myself, trust the timing, and keep moving.

That's the real skill. Not optimism. Not pretending the hard weeks don't sting. Just refusing to let one disappointing chapter write the ending before the story is finished.
